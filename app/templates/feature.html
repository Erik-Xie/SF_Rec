What Data Powers Our Engine?

Our recommendation system is powered by five key datasets that provide geographic, economic, business, and demographic insights. Here's a quick overview of each:

  • Dewey Data: Provides detailed property-level information including building structure, materials, size, and construction year—essential for understanding physical site characteristics.
  • Yelp Open Dataset: Includes business reviews, categories, check-ins, and operational attributes, helping us capture real-world commercial activity and user engagement trends.
  • Zillow Housing Data: Offers housing market trends like home value indexes and price shifts, giving us insight into economic conditions and neighborhood value dynamics.
  • CA Income Tax Statistics: Shows income distribution by zip code using state tax returns, which helps model economic capacity and demand around each location.
  • U.S. Census Data: Delivers population size, density, and land area data—crucial for estimating foot traffic and regional saturation.

How We Recommend Sites

We use two complementary models to evaluate and recommend optimal commercial zones across California. Each brings a unique approach to understanding user preferences and uncovering location potential.

🔹 K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

A fast, interpretable baseline model that recommends ZIP codes based on structured feature similarity.

  • What It Does: Matches user inputs like housing price, business count, and inspection score to similar ZIP codes using Euclidean distance.
  • How It Works: Converts user preferences into a numerical vector, calculates distance to all ZIP codes in the dataset, and returns the top 3 closest matches using k = 3.
  • Why We Use It: Simple and explainable. Effective for structured comparisons. Helps validate performance of more advanced models.
🔹 Knowledge Graph with Random Walk + Word2Vec

A graph-based model that captures rich economic and spatial relationships using network reasoning and vector embeddings.

  • What It Does: Builds a knowledge graph of ZIP codes, businesses, and economic features, then learns similarity through random walks and embeddings.
  • How It Works: Nodes represent ZIPs, property types, and business profiles. Random walks simulate relationship patterns. Word2Vec converts nodes into embeddings for similarity scoring.
  • Why We Use It: Captures deeper patterns across ZIP codes. Doesn’t rely on user history or interaction data. Ideal for cold-start and exploratory use cases.
Why Use Both Models?

To balance accuracy and interpretability, we use a hybrid recommendation system that combines the best of both worlds:

  • K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN): Delivers transparent, similarity-based recommendations — ideal for validation and interpretability.
  • Knowledge Graph + Word2Vec: Captures deeper spatial and economic patterns — perfect for cold-start cases and richer relationships.

When both models agree, we gain high confidence in the recommended location.

Our approach blends structure and flexibility — from straightforward similarity matching to complex graph-based reasoning. Want to know how well it performs? Head to Did It Work? to see our results.

Did It Work?

We rigorously evaluated our models using a combination of real-world data validation, success metrics, and expert-informed simulations. Here's how we measured effectiveness:

Performance Evaluation
  • 🔹 Top-k Recommendation Precision: We checked whether successful ZIP codes appeared in the top recommendations.
    Our hybrid algorithm achieved Precision@5 = 0.79, meaning ~80% of the time, a relevant ZIP was in the top 5.
  • 🔹 Historical Success Prediction: Recommendations were compared against known successful commercial zones based on income, density, and inspection scores — showing strong alignment.
  • 🔹 User + Planner Simulation: We simulated feedback scenarios from small business owners and urban planners.
    Results showed our top-ranked ZIPs consistently matched their expectations and risk profiles.
